# Business Strategy & Performance Turnaround

> **Turning around a loss-making consulting practice through business development, client retention, delivery excellence, resource optimisation and commercial discipline.**

---

## Executive Summary

I joined **Protiviti Qatar in August 2014** as a Manager with a specific objective: to contribute to the growth and turnaround of a consulting practice that was facing a challenging financial position.

I deliberately viewed the role as a turnaround opportunity rather than simply another consulting position. The challenge was to contribute to sustainable growth by combining **business development, high-quality client delivery, account expansion, resource deployment and commercial management**.

During approximately **18 months**, I developed or contributed to approximately **15–20 business proposals**, managed major client engagements, supported significant account expansion, participated in resource placement and billing management, and contributed to the development of new revenue opportunities.

The experience demonstrated that a consulting practice cannot be turned around through sales alone. Sustainable improvement requires an integrated model connecting:

**Business Development → Client Wins → Quality Delivery → Client Retention → Resource Utilisation → Billing → Profitability**

---

# Business Context

Protiviti Qatar operated in a highly competitive consulting market, competing for major assignments against established international consulting and accounting firms.

When I joined in **August 2014**, the practice was experiencing financial difficulties.

Rather than viewing this solely as a revenue-generation problem, I approached it as a broader **practice-performance challenge**.

The objective was to strengthen the practice through five interconnected areas:

1. **Business Development**
2. **Client Relationship & Account Expansion**
3. **Delivery Excellence**
4. **Resource Deployment**
5. **Commercial & Billing Discipline**

This required balancing business development with significant hands-on engagement responsibilities.

---

1. Business Development

A significant part of my role involved developing new business opportunities.

During approximately 18 months, I developed or contributed to approximately 15–20 proposals across different clients, industries and service areas.

These included opportunities involving:

Internal Audit
Risk Management
Governance
Project Assurance
Operational Reviews
IT / Technology Audit
Business Reviews
Specialist advisory assignments

The approach to proposals was not limited to responding to an RFP.

I sought to understand:

What problem the client was trying to solve.
Why the problem mattered to management.
What risks were involved.
What deliverables would create value.
What resources were required.
How the engagement could be delivered effectively.
How Protiviti could differentiate its approach.

This helped connect commercial development with actual client needs.

2. Client Retention & Account Expansion

One of the strongest opportunities for practice growth came from existing clients.

A major example was Barwa Real Estate Company, where I served as Project Manager across two audit cycles.

The engagement demonstrated the relationship between:

Quality Delivery → Client Confidence → Contract Expansion

The first-year project value was approximately:

QR 750,000

A variation order subsequently increased the engagement to approximately:

QR 1 million

The following year's contract was approximately:

QR 1.5 million

The increase reflected client satisfaction with the performance of the Protiviti team and the ability to deliver a significant volume of audit work.

The engagement ultimately involved approximately 24 audit reports across two audit cycles.

This experience reinforced an important consulting principle:

Successful delivery is one of the strongest forms of business development.

3. Major Client Delivery

A key differentiator in my approach was that I remained directly involved in execution.

I did not operate purely as a business-development manager.

I continued to manage and execute major engagements while simultaneously contributing to practice development.

Barwa Real Estate Company

As Project Manager, I:

Led the Protiviti team.
Managed approximately 24 reports across two audit cycles.
Directly executed and finalised finance audit reports.
Performed quality assurance over team deliverables.
Reviewed corporate, IT and project audits.
Supported management discussions.
Finalised reports for senior internal-audit review.
Al Fardan Group

Following completion of the Barwa assignment, I took responsibility as Group Internal Audit Manager for Al Fardan.

The assignment involved taking over a portfolio of audits requiring significant restructuring and execution.

During approximately four to five months, I managed and finalised more than 16 reports.

The assignment demonstrated the ability to take over a challenging client situation, establish control over the audit portfolio and deliver a substantial volume of work within a compressed timeframe.

Milaha / Qatar Navigation

I also took over an incomplete assignment at Milaha / Qatar Navigation early during my Protiviti Qatar tenure.

The assignment required significant rework and restructuring before it could be finalised.

The completed report was subsequently presented to senior leadership, including the Chairman-level governance structure.

This demonstrated the ability to:

Diagnose an incomplete engagement.
Rework the audit approach.
Complete outstanding procedures.
Finalise the report.
Communicate the results at senior-management level.
ASTAD

I led a project audit involving a QR 300 million greenhouse project for ASTAD.

The observations were discussed with the project's Senior Manager and COO.

The assignment strengthened my experience in:

Project audit.
Capital-project assurance.
Financial review.
Operational review.
Senior stakeholder communication.

This demonstrated the importance of maintaining visibility over:

Staff utilisation.
Billing opportunities.
Resource capacity.

Effective resource management allowed the practice to convert available capability into additional commercial opportunities.

Appropriate scope management.
Additional work where justified.
Effective commercial control.
6. Client Relationship Management

Client relationships were central to the approach.

My focus was to build relationships based on three principles:

Credibility

Understand the client's business and risks rather than simply execute a standard audit programme.

Delivery

Produce work that management considers useful and actionable.

Responsiveness

Address issues quickly and maintain communication with senior stakeholders.

This approach supported both client retention and opportunities for account expansion.
