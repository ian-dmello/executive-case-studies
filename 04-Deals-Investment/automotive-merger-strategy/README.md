# Strategic Merger Evaluation for an Automotive Group

**Client:** Teyseer Group (Confidential Automotive Companies)  
**Employer:** Teyseer Group  
**Role:** Group Internal Audit Manager  
**Duration:** 2020–2021

---

## Executive Summary

The Group Internal Audit function was requested by executive management to provide an independent strategic assessment of a proposed merger between two profitable automotive businesses within the Group.

The proposal involved merging the Group's automobile dealership business with its automotive services company. While the proposal had support from one management team, the management of the second company opposed the transaction. Executive management required an objective assessment of whether the proposed merger would create sustainable shareholder value.

I led the strategic evaluation, analysing multiple restructuring alternatives, assessing strategic fit, business models, operational synergies, financial implications, organisational culture, and long-term competitive positioning.

Following a comprehensive analysis, I recommended **not proceeding with the merger**, concluding that the expected synergies were insufficient to justify the strategic, operational, and organisational risks. The recommendation was presented by the Group Internal Auditor to the Deputy Chairman and was accepted following detailed discussion.

---

# Business Context

The proposed transaction involved two successful companies operating within the Group's automotive sector.

The first company operated as the authorised dealer for Suzuki and Peugeot vehicles and was primarily engaged in vehicle sales and spare parts distribution.

The second company operated fuel stations, vehicle servicing centres, maintenance workshops, vehicle cleaning services, and leased commercial properties.

Although both businesses operated within the automotive ecosystem, executive management questioned whether combining the two organisations would generate operational efficiencies and accelerate future growth.

---

# Executive Challenge

Executive management requested an independent assessment addressing the following questions:

- Would the merger create long-term shareholder value?
- Which merger structure would maximise value?
- Would operational and financial synergies justify the transaction?
- Would the merged organisation be strategically stronger than the existing independent businesses?

---

# My Role

As Group Internal Audit Manager, I was responsible for conducting the independent strategic evaluation.

My responsibilities included:

- Evaluating alternative merger structures.
- Assessing strategic alignment between both businesses.
- Analysing business models and competitive positioning.
- Assessing operational and financial synergies.
- Evaluating organisational culture and integration risks.
- Preparing an executive recommendation paper.
- Supporting executive management in strategic decision-making.

# Strategic Alternatives Evaluated

Four strategic alternatives were evaluated.

| Alternative | Assessment |
|-------------|------------|
| Automotive dealership acquires the services company | Evaluated |
| Automotive services company acquires the dealership business | Evaluated |
| Both companies remain separate legal entities under a holding/subsidiary structure | Evaluated |
| Continue operating as independent companies | **Recommended** |

---

# Strategic Assessment Methodology

The engagement adopted a structured strategy consulting approach.

## Business Model Analysis

Compared:

- Revenue models
- Cost structures
- Capital intensity
- Labour intensity
- Operating leverage
- Profitability drivers

The analysis concluded that the two businesses operated fundamentally different commercial models.

---

## Strategic Fit Assessment

Evaluated:

- Competitive strategy
- Market positioning
- Growth strategy
- Customer segments
- Value proposition

The dealership business pursued a **product differentiation strategy**, whereas the services company primarily competed through **cost leadership**, resulting in limited strategic alignment.

---

## Synergy Assessment

Evaluated potential:

- Revenue synergies
- Cost reductions
- Cross-selling opportunities
- Procurement efficiencies
- Shared services
- Operational integration

The analysis concluded that meaningful synergies were limited and unlikely to justify the complexity of integration.

---

## Organisational & Cultural Assessment

Compared:

- Workforce composition
- Organisational culture
- Management structures
- Decision-making approaches
- Customer profiles

Significant cultural differences increased the risk of post-merger integration challenges.

---

## Portfolio & Growth Assessment

Evaluated:

- Relative size of both companies
- Expansion plans
- Market positioning
- Future scalability
- Management complexity

The proposed merged entity would become substantially larger than every other business within the Group, increasing organisational complexity without delivering proportionate strategic benefits.

---

# Key Findings

## Limited Strategic Alignment

Although both companies operated within the automotive sector, they served different customer segments, employed different business models, and pursued different competitive strategies.

---

## Limited Synergy Potential

Expected benefits from cross-selling, procurement, and cost reduction were insufficient to justify integration costs and organisational disruption.

---

## Cultural Differences

The dealership business employed a highly skilled commercial workforce serving premium customers, while the services company relied primarily on operational and technical staff.

The differing organisational cultures increased integration risk.

---

## Business Model Differences

The dealership business was capital-intensive and product-focused.

The services company was labour-intensive and service-oriented.

The underlying economics and operating models differed significantly.

---

## Growth Considerations

Maintaining separate businesses would allow both organisations to continue pursuing independent expansion strategies without compromising operational focus.

---

# Recommendation

Following evaluation of all four alternatives, the preferred recommendation was **not to proceed with the merger**.

The recommendation was supported by:

- Limited strategic fit.
- Minimal operational synergies.
- Different competitive strategies.
- Cultural incompatibility.
- Increased organisational complexity.
- Preservation of independent growth opportunities.
- Strong standalone financial performance of both companies.
