# Financial Reporting Turnaround & Statutory Audit Review

**Client:** Orbit Electronics & Communication (Middle East)  
**Employer:** Orbit Electronics & Communication  
**Role:** Finance & Audit Consultant / Finance Manager  
**Duration:** 2016–2017

---

## Executive Summary

I joined Orbit Electronics & Communication as Finance Manager on a short-term transformation assignment with three primary objectives:

- Restructure the Finance Department.
- Conduct a management audit of the business.
- Establish a sustainable finance function before leaving to pursue my MBA at the Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad.

One of the first assignments assigned by the Chief Executive Officer was to independently review the statutory financial statements and tax computation prepared by the company's external auditors.

The CEO believed that the reported profit was significantly overstated and requested an independent assessment before approving the financial statements and settling the shareholder tax liability.

My review uncovered significant departures from accrual accounting principles, inadequate statutory audit procedures and material misstatements in the financial statements. After presenting documentary evidence to the statutory auditors, they accepted the findings and revised the audited financial statements, substantially reducing taxable profits and shareholder tax liabilities.

---

# Business Context

The company operated as a mid-sized contracting business executing projects for government and private sector clients.

During a period of declining oil prices and slowing economic activity, management questioned the unusually high profits reported by the external auditors.

The CEO believed the reported results were inconsistent with both market conditions and the company's operational performance and therefore commissioned an independent review before approving the financial statements.

---

# Executive Challenge

The engagement required answering four critical questions:

- Were the audited financial statements materially correct?
- Had the statutory auditors exercised adequate professional diligence?
- Were the company's accounting records compliant with accrual accounting principles?
- What changes were required to improve the finance function and internal controls?

# Approach & Methodology

## Phase 1 — Business & Process Review

Before reviewing the financial statements, I conducted an internal audit covering the company's major business processes to understand operational risks and control weaknesses.

This review identified:

- Weak financial controls.
- Fraudulent fuel reimbursement claims.
- Inadequate segregation of duties.
- Weak ERP controls.
- Lack of audit trails.
- Inappropriate system access rights.

The investigation into fuel claims resulted in disciplinary action against a senior manager after fraudulent invoices were identified.

---

## Phase 2 — ERP Control Assessment

The company operated on Tally ERP.

The review highlighted several weaknesses:

- Manual journal entries could be passed without adequate controls.
- User access rights were inadequately restricted.
- No effective audit trail existed for accounting changes.
- Limited system controls over financial reporting.

These weaknesses increased the risk of accounting errors and financial manipulation.

---

## Phase 3 — Financial Statement Review

I performed a detailed review of:

- Bank reconciliations.
- Accruals.
- Provisions.
- Project accounting.
- Ledger scrutiny.
- Billing documentation.
- Contractor payments.
- Supporting invoices.
- Financial statement disclosures.

Rather than relying solely on ledger balances, every significant accounting entry was traced back to its supporting documentation.

---

## Phase 4 — Accounting Analysis

The review identified several material accounting issues.

### Incorrect Matching of Revenue and Costs

Revenue relating to completed projects had been recognised during the financial year.

However, significant subcontractor costs relating to the same revenue had been recorded during the following financial year.

This materially overstated current-year profitability.

---

### Failure to Apply Accrual Accounting

Several significant liabilities had not been recognised despite services already being received.

Instead, expenditure was recognised only when payments were made.

The accounting records therefore reflected a predominantly cash-based approach rather than accrual accounting.

---

### Ledger Classification Errors

Detailed ledger scrutiny identified expenditure recorded under incorrect accounting heads, reducing the reliability of management reporting.

---

### Unrecorded Liabilities

Review of supplier documentation identified several invoices that should have been recognised before year-end but had not been recorded in the accounting system.

---

## Phase 5 — Independent Validation

I presented the documentary evidence to the statutory auditors.

Following detailed discussions and verification of supporting documentation, the auditors acknowledged that:

- Supporting invoices had not been adequately verified.
- Key substantive audit procedures had not been performed.
- Excessive reliance had been placed on the finance department's accounting records because of the long-standing client relationship.

The statutory auditors subsequently revised the audited financial statements based on the evidence presented.

# Results Delivered

- Identified material misstatements in audited financial statements.
- Corrected significant breaches of accrual accounting principles.
- Identified unrecorded liabilities and project accounting errors.
- Improved the accuracy of financial reporting.
- Reduced shareholder tax liabilities through correction of overstated profits.
- Strengthened confidence in financial reporting through independent verification.
- Completed a management audit identifying multiple operational control weaknesses.
- Detected fraudulent fuel reimbursement practices resulting in disciplinary action.
- Initiated restructuring of the finance function and improvements to internal controls.

---

# Business Impact

The revised financial statements produced a substantially lower and more accurate profit figure.

The CEO confirmed that the revised results aligned closely with his own commercial expectations based on prevailing market conditions and the decline in government infrastructure spending caused by falling oil prices.

The revised accounts were accepted by management and formed the basis for the final shareholder tax computation, avoiding significant excess tax payments.
