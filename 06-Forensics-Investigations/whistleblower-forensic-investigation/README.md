# Whistleblower Investigation & Executive Misconduct Review

**Client:** Confidential Group Company (Mechanical Equipment & Consumer Travel Products)  
**Employer:** Teyseer Group  
**Role:** Group Internal Audit Manager – Investigation Lead  
**Duration:** 2020–2021

---

## Executive Summary

The Group Internal Audit function received instructions from executive management to investigate allegations raised through a whistleblower complaint concerning the Senior Sales Manager of one of the Group's trading companies.

The investigation was initiated following declining revenue, recurring operating losses, and multiple allegations relating to conflicts of interest, misuse of company resources, related-party transactions, and abuse of authority. The appointment of a new General Manager uncovered additional evidence that warranted an independent forensic investigation.

As Investigation Lead, supported by one audit professional, I completed the investigation within approximately three weeks. The engagement combined forensic interviewing, commercial analytics, document review, procurement analysis, governance assessment, and fraud risk evaluation. The investigation concluded with a confidential report supported by documentary evidence, which resulted in disciplinary action by executive management.

---

# Business Context

The business imported and distributed mechanical equipment and consumer travel products within Qatar.

For approximately three years, the Senior Sales Manager exercised significant operational authority in the absence of a General Manager. During this period, revenue declined, profitability deteriorated, and several experienced commercial employees left the organisation.

Following the appointment of a new General Manager, executive management requested Group Internal Audit to independently investigate allegations received through the whistleblower mechanism.

---

# Executive Challenge

Executive management required an independent investigation to determine whether:

- Conflicts of interest existed.
- Related-party transactions had occurred.
- Company assets had been misused.
- Procurement and pricing decisions were appropriate.
- Governance failures contributed to deteriorating financial performance.
- Disciplinary action was warranted.

---

# My Role

As Investigation Lead, I was responsible for:

- Planning and directing the investigation.
- Developing the investigation methodology.
- Supervising investigative procedures.
- Conducting interviews.
- Performing forensic data analysis.
- Reviewing commercial and procurement transactions.
- Assessing governance and control failures.
- Preparing the confidential investigation report.
- Presenting findings to the Group Internal Auditor.

# Investigation Methodology

The investigation followed a structured forensic methodology.

## Phase 1 – Fraud Risk Assessment

The allegations were initially assessed using the **Fraud Diamond Framework**, evaluating:

- Opportunity
- Motivation
- Rationalisation
- Capability

This helped prioritise investigative procedures and identify potential fraud indicators.

---

## Phase 2 – Documentary Review

Reviewed:

- Personnel files
- Expense claims
- Procurement documentation
- Marketing expenditure
- Sales promotion expenditure
- Administrative expenses
- Customer discounts
- Free issue approvals
- Overseas travel approvals
- Delegation of Authority compliance
- Purchase transactions

---

## Phase 3 – Commercial Analytics

Performed analytical reviews covering:

- Customer profitability
- Gross margin analysis
- Pricing exceptions
- Below-cost sales
- Discount analysis
- Free item analysis
- Procurement pricing
- Inventory purchases

Outlier transactions were identified for detailed investigation.

---

## Phase 4 – Interviews

Conducted structured interviews with:

- Senior management
- Commercial personnel
- Finance personnel
- Employees who previously reported to the Senior Sales Manager

The interviews helped corroborate documentary evidence and establish behavioural patterns.

---

## Phase 5 – Hypothesis Development

Developed investigative hypotheses based on:

- Conflict-of-interest indicators
- Concentration of authority
- Weak internal controls
- Related-party relationships
- Vendor associations
- Competitor relationships
- Delegation of Authority violations

Each hypothesis was subsequently tested using documentary evidence and transaction analysis.

---

## Phase 6 – Evidence Validation

Evidence was independently corroborated using:

- Accounting records
- Supporting documentation
- Sales transactions
- Procurement records
- Personnel records
- System-generated reports

Where evidence was insufficient to support an allegation, conclusions were revised accordingly.

---

# Key Findings

## Related-Party Transactions

Identified sales to businesses connected to close relatives of the Senior Sales Manager involving:

- Significant commercial discounts.
- Free products.
- Sales below normal commercial margins.

The cumulative commercial benefit exceeded **QAR 1 million**.

---

## Conflict of Interest

Identified commercial relationships between the Senior Sales Manager and a competing organisation employing a close family member, creating significant conflict-of-interest concerns.

---

## Misuse of Company Assets

Identified use of company vehicles, employees, and overtime payments for personal purposes unrelated to company business.

---

## Commercial Expenditure

Identified substantial entertainment and promotional expenditure without corresponding improvements in revenue or profitability.

---

## Procurement Governance

Identified procurement of compressors valued at approximately **QAR 1.5 million** that remained unsold for an extended period due to inadequate demand assessment and procurement planning.

The initial investigation considered potential procurement fraud; however, after evaluating available evidence, this hypothesis was not substantiated. The final report concluded that the issue resulted from inadequate procurement planning and weak commercial oversight rather than fraudulent conduct.

---

## Governance

Identified repeated non-compliance with the Delegation of Authority framework, including:

- Significant discounts.
- Below-cost sales.
- Free product approvals.

These transactions had not received the approvals required under the company's governance framework.

---

# Report Finalisation

To ensure the investigation would withstand legal scrutiny:

- Every finding was supported by documentary evidence.
- Analytical calculations were fully documented.
- Evidence sources were cross-referenced.
- The report underwent review by the General Manager and Group Internal Auditor.
- Group Legal reviewed the report to ensure compliance with Qatari labour legislation.
- The employee was provided an opportunity to review and respond to the findings before management reached its decision.

---

# Results Delivered

- Completed the forensic investigation within approximately **three weeks**.
- Applied the Fraud Diamond framework to structure investigative procedures.
- Identified related-party transactions exceeding **QAR 1 million**.
- Identified procurement decisions involving approximately **QAR 1.5 million** of slow-moving inventory.
- Strengthened governance by identifying significant Delegation of Authority violations.
- Produced a legally supported investigation report reviewed by Group Legal.
- Findings were accepted by executive management and resulted in the termination of the Senior Sales Manager.

# Professional Judgment & Ethical Considerations

Throughout the investigation, maintaining objectivity and evidentiary integrity remained paramount.

Key principles applied included:

- Treating allegations as unproven until supported by evidence.
- Revising investigative conclusions where evidence was insufficient to substantiate an allegation.
- Respecting confidentiality throughout the investigation.
- Ensuring findings were supported by verifiable documentary evidence.
- Providing the employee with an opportunity to respond before management reached a final decision.
- Balancing investigative thoroughness with legal and cultural considerations when determining what information was relevant to the investigation.
